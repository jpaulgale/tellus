Prototype with Manhattan Community Board 2

A text box that fills in the right form.

Residents write in plain words. Tell Us matches the message to the right form, whether that's the community board's own intake or a city form like a 311 service request. It fills in what it can, asks for the required fields it can't, and sends a complete record to the right place.

Speaking up shouldn't take research.

Today, a neighbor with a problem has to work out the system before anyone hears them.

Today

  • Which community board covers this area?
  • Which of the board's 12+ committees handles this?
  • Is it even a community board issue, or is it 311, DSNY, NYPD?
  • OK, I know who this should go to, but which form or email do I send it to, and what information do they require?

With Tell Us

Just say what's going on.

Tell Us decides who and where, extracts all of the info it can from your input, and asks for any required information you missed, in real time.

From a sentence to a structured record.

The text box isn't the end product. Behind it is a real form with required fields, and what comes out is a structured record ready for the office that handles it.

The board decides what its form asks.

Every destination is a form definition: its fields, which ones are required, the allowed answers, and where it goes. Tell Us just helps residents fill it in.

Set by the community board

The board's own intake form

For issues the board weighs in on: licenses, street redesigns, development, parks, schools.

  • CommitteeRequiredChosen from the board's own committee list
  • Type of issueRequiredNoise, hours, crowds, outdoor dining, safety…
  • What you want the board to doRequiredOppose, add conditions, support, or just let the board know
  • Name and emailRequiredSo the board can reply
  • Wants to speak at a meetingOptional

What a form definition looks like

Forms live in a plain JSON file a board or its tech partner can edit. This is the crossing guard form, trimmed:

{
  "name": "Crossing Guard Request",
  "agency": "NYPD",
  "complaint_type": "School Crossing Guard",
  "route": "A request for a school crossing guard at an intersection near a school",
  "contact_required": true,
  "fields": [
    { "label": "Request", "required": true,
      "choices": ["New crossing guard at this corner", "Crossing guard is absent", "Need a guard for more hours"] },
    { "label": "School name", "required": true },
    { "label": "Time of day", "required": true,
      "choices": ["Morning drop-off", "Afternoon dismissal", "Both drop-off and dismissal"] }
  ]
}

Each message lands where it can be acted on.

Not everything is the board's to handle. Tell Us checks the topic and the address, then picks the form and the destination.

Better for both sides.

For residents

  • No hunting for the right email, committee or city form.
  • Asked only for the details that make the message actionable.
  • Sure it reached the people who can do something about it.

For boards and their tech partners

  • Intake arrives complete, on the board's own form, sorted to a committee.
  • 311 issues and other districts' mail go where they belong instead of the board's inbox.
  • Every submission is a structured record you can store, forward or analyze.
